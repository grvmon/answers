The builder gave possession without an OC. What now?

Arvind Patidar Co-Founder
Published: 17 Oct 2026 • 7 min read
Share:

Direct answer: Under RERA, obtaining the occupancy certificate (or completion certificate under local law) is the promoter's duty, and conveyance timelines are tied to it. Taking keys without an OC does not wipe those duties. Ask for the OC in writing, check the Karnataka RERA project page, and if needed file under section 31 with your lawyer.

Verify with your lawyer: Possession without OC mixes local building law, RERA duties and your agreement. This page summarises the Act text only. Have your own property lawyer review your papers before you sign a handover letter, pay final dues or file a complaint.

Say you have the keys, a "possession letter" and a maintenance demand, but no occupancy certificate. The RERA Act treats physical handover and the promoter's duty to obtain the certificate as two separate things, and this page walks through what the Act says about each.

  2. Whose job is it to get the OC?

What is an occupancy certificate under RERA?

Section 2(zf) defines "occupancy certificate" as the certificate, by whatever name, issued by the competent authority that permits occupation of a building under local laws and provides for civic infrastructure such as water, sanitation and electricity (RERA Act PDF).

The Act does not create a separate RERA "OC form". It points to whatever certificate the local law requires and binds the promoter to obtain it. Our explainer on why OC and CC matter covers the document types.

Whose job is it to get the OC?

Section 11(4)(b) says the promoter shall "be responsible to obtain the completion certificate or the occupancy certificate, or both, as applicable, from the relevant competent authority as per local laws… and to make it available to the allottees individually or to the association of allottees".

That duty sits with the promoter. A possession letter from the sales team does not replace it. Section 11(4)(a) also keeps the promoter responsible for Act and agreement obligations until conveyance of the apartments and common areas is done.

How do OC and conveyance connect?

Section 17(1) requires the promoter to execute a registered conveyance deed in favour of the allottee (with undivided proportionate title in the common areas to the association) and to hand over physical possession within the period set by local law and the sanctioned plans.

The proviso is the OC hook: "in the absence of any local law, conveyance deed… shall be carried out by the promoter within three months from date of issue of occupancy certificate."

Section 17(2) then requires the promoter, after obtaining the OC and handing over possession, to hand over the necessary documents and plans, including common areas, to the association as per local law. Where there is no local law, the proviso sets thirty days "after obtaining the completion certificate".

Section 19(10) puts a matching duty on you: take physical possession within two months of the OC issued for your apartment. Section 19(11) says you must participate in registering the conveyance under section 17(1).

So the Act's timeline language assumes an OC exists. Handing over keys without one leaves the statutory clock incomplete.

Does taking keys waive my rights?

No provision in the Act says that accepting physical possession cancels section 11(4)(b) or section 17. What you sign at handover can still matter in practice. Before you sign a full-and-final or "no claim" letter:

  1. Ask for a copy of the OC (or a written statement that it is not yet issued, with a target date).
  2. Cross-check the project's page on rera.karnataka.gov.in for uploaded approvals and status (how to check a project on Karnataka RERA).
  3. Keep the possession letter, demand notes and photos of incomplete common areas.
  4. Have your lawyer read any indemnity or waiver language before you sign.

If the agreement promised possession of a duly completed apartment by a date, and the promoter cannot give that possession as agreed, section 18 may apply. Section 18(1) lets you withdraw with refund plus interest and compensation, or stay and claim interest for every month of delay, when the promoter fails to complete or is unable to give possession "in accordance with the terms of the agreement for sale" by the stated date. Whether "possession without OC" counts as that failure depends on the facts, so have your lawyer apply the agreement language to your case.

What practical problems follow an OC-less handover?

  • Your home loan. Ask your lender in writing what it needs before the last tranche is released. Our page on home loan disbursement for under-construction flats explains how tranches work.
  • Utilities and khata. Ask the promoter how the permanent power and water connections and the khata will be applied for, and which certificate each office has asked for. See permanent BESCOM power for a new flat and BWSSB connection for a new apartment.
  • Conveyance and resale. Where no local law sets the period, the section 17(1) proviso counts the three-month conveyance window from the OC date. Without an OC, that date has not arrived, and any later buyer's lawyer will ask about it.
  • Association handover. Section 17(2) document handover is framed after the OC. Our note on apartment association handover in Karnataka covers the governance side.

None of these practical issues cancel the Act duties above. They are why the certificate matters day to day.

What steps should I take now?

  1. Write to the promoter (email plus registered post if needed) asking for the OC number, issuing authority, date, and a copy. Quote section 11(4)(b).
  2. Check the RERA project page for uploaded certificates and the declared completion date under section 4.
  3. Read your agreement for the possession date, what "possession" means, and any OC-linked clauses. See what to check in the sale agreement.
  4. Talk to other allottees. If the whole tower is waiting on the OC, a joint letter from the association or a buyer group puts the same question to the promoter once.
  5. File with Karnataka RERA under section 31 if the promoter does not obtain or share the certificate. Procedure is in how to file a Karnataka RERA complaint.
  6. Do not stop documenting. Keep every payment receipt, WhatsApp promise and site photo.

What this page does not cover

Local building bye-laws, BBMP/planning authority OC application forms, and any state-specific circular on temporary occupation are outside the central Act text used here. Your lawyer maps those to your project. This page also does not quote interest rates; prescribed rates under section 18 come from the rules your lawyer cites.

Frequently asked questions

Can a builder legally hand over keys without an OC?

The Act makes the promoter responsible for obtaining the OC or CC and for conveyance timelines tied to it. Whether a particular handover without OC breaches your agreement is a case-specific legal question. Ask your lawyer before you treat the keys as full compliance.

I already signed a possession letter. Have I lost my claim?

Signing a handover note does not automatically erase section 11(4)(b) or section 17 duties in the Act. What you signed can still affect strategy. Show the letter to your lawyer before you file or settle.

Is a completion certificate the same as an OC?

Section 11(4)(b) refers to "the completion certificate or the occupancy certificate, or both, as applicable" under local laws. Names and forms vary by authority. Confirm which document your project needs under Bengaluru rules.

Does section 19(10) force me to take possession without an OC?

Section 19(10) requires you to take physical possession within two months of the OC issued for the apartment. If no OC has been issued, that two-month clock has not started under the Act's wording.

Can I claim section 18 interest if I took the keys but still have no OC?

Section 18 turns on failure to complete or give possession in accordance with the agreement (or on registration suspension/revocation). Whether your facts fit is for your lawyer. Taking keys does not by itself prove or disprove a section 18 claim.
