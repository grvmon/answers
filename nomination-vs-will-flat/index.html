Does a housing society nomination replace a will?

Arvind Patidar
Co-Founder · Operations, CEX & Tech • Published: 17 Oct 2026 • 7 min read
Share:

Direct answer: No. In the nomination vs will property question, the will decides who owns your flat; a nomination only tells a society, association or bank whom to deal with after your death. The Supreme Court has held that a nominee isn't the owner and heirs keep their rights. To leave the flat to someone specific, write a valid will.

What are the key takeaways?

  • A nominee is a caretaker, not an owner, unless they're also your heir or named in your will.
  • Supreme Court, 2016: a co-operative society must transfer membership to the nominee, but that has "no relevance" to title between heirs (Indrani Wahi).
  • Supreme Court, 2023: nomination doesn't override succession law (Shakti Yezdani, on shares).
  • A will needs two witnesses (Indian Succession Act s.63). Registering it is optional but helps.
  • Our KEEP check: know your heirs, execute a will, enter nominations, place the papers.

Many flat owners fill in a nomination form with the association, the bank or the loan insurer and assume that's settled. It isn't. This page explains the difference and shows, with numbers, what happens when there's a nomination but no will.

What does a nomination actually do?

It tells an institution whom to hand something to, so it isn't stuck waiting for court papers. It doesn't decide who ends up owning it.

  • Co-operative housing society. In Indrani Wahi v Registrar of Co-operative Societies (Supreme Court, 10 March 2016, under West Bengal's law), the court said the society must transfer the share to a valid nominee. It added that this "would have no relevance to the issue of title" between heirs, who could still pursue their claims.
  • Shares and securities. In Shakti Yezdani v Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar (14 December 2023), the court held that nomination doesn't make the nominee the absolute owner or override succession law.
  • Apartment associations in Bengaluru. Most flats here are owned outright under a registered sale deed, with an owners' association managing common areas. An association's nomination form is an instruction to the association, not a transfer of the flat.

Worked example 1: a nominee but no will

Illustrative. A Hindu father owns a ₹1.2 crore flat in his sole name and has named his son as nominee with the society. He dies without a will. His widow, son and daughter survive him; his mother died before him.

Under Section 10 of the Hindu Succession Act, the widow, each son and each daughter take one share:

Nomination only Will leaving the flat to the son
Widow 1/3, ₹40 lakh 0
Son (nominee) 1/3, ₹40 lakh 100%, ₹1.2 crore
Daughter 1/3, ₹40 lakh 0

With only the nomination, the son may hold the membership but owns a third. Only the will gives him the flat.

Worked example 2: a jointly owned flat

Illustrative. A husband and wife own a flat 50:50. The husband dies without a will, leaving his widow, a son and a daughter (his mother died before him). Only his half passes by succession.

Owner Own share From husband's 50% (÷ 3) Total
Widow 50% 16.67% 66.67%
Son 0 16.67% 16.67%
Daughter 0 16.67% 16.67%

Joint ownership doesn't automatically mean the survivor gets everything. If that's what you both want, each of you needs a will saying so. If one of the heirs lives abroad or can't sign, selling later gets harder; our answer on inherited flat documents covers that.

What makes a will valid?

  • Signed by you, in a way that shows you mean it as your will.
  • Attested by two or more witnesses, each of whom saw you sign (or heard you acknowledge it) and signed in your presence (Indian Succession Act 1925, s.63).
  • Registration is optional under the Registration Act 1908, but a registered will is harder to dispute and easier to find.
  • Probate is no longer compulsory anywhere in India after the Repealing and Amending Act 2025 removed Section 213 (Indian Express, 28 Dec 2025, news). Banks and registrars may still ask for it.

How does the KEEP check work?

  1. Know your heirs. Who inherits if there's no will, under your personal law?
  2. Execute a will. Two witnesses; describe the flat by its deed details.
  3. Enter nominations. Name the same person with the association, bank and insurers, so the paperwork matches the will.
  4. Place the papers. Register the will or keep it safe, and tell your executor where it is.

What should be on your checklist?

  • Ownership checked on the sale deed: sole, joint, and in what shares
  • Heirs listed under your personal law
  • Will drafted with the flat's deed and schedule details
  • Two witnesses who aren't beneficiaries
  • Will registered or safely stored, with the executor told
  • Nominations with the association, bank and insurer match the will
  • Will reviewed after marriage, birth, death, or a new property

Frequently asked questions

Does a nominee become the owner of a flat?

Not by being a nominee. The Supreme Court held in 2016 that a society's transfer to a nominee has no bearing on title between heirs, and in 2023 that nomination doesn't override succession law.

Which wins, the will or the nomination?

The will, if it's valid. A nominee who isn't the beneficiary under the will holds things for the people entitled to them under the will or succession law.

Do I need to register my will in Karnataka?

No. Registering a will is optional under the Registration Act 1908. It does make the will harder to challenge and easier to locate later.

How many witnesses does a will need?

At least two, under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act. Each must see you sign or hear you acknowledge your signature, and sign in your presence.

If a flat is jointly owned, does the survivor get it all?

Not automatically. Only the deceased's share passes, by will or succession. In our example, a widow ends up with 66.67% and each child with 16.67%.

It's our four-step plan: know your heirs, execute a valid will, enter matching nominations everywhere, and place the papers where your executor can find them.
