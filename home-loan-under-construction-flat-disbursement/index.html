How does a home loan for an under-construction flat work (tranches and pre-EMI)?

By Gaurav Mongia, acre&key · Published 17 Oct 2026 · Updated 17 Oct 2026

By Gaurav Mongia, acre&key · Published 16 Oct 2026 · Updated 16 Oct 2026

Direct answer: Banks usually sanction a loan against an approved under-construction project, then release money in stages tied to construction progress and your payment plan. Until full disbursement, you typically pay interest on the amount already released (often called pre-EMI). Loan-to-value, rates and stage lists vary by bank, so confirm them in your sanction letter.

Verify with your lawyer: This page is general guidance from the RERA Act and related public sources, not legal advice. Have your own property lawyer review the documents for your specific flat before you sign or pay.

Verify with your CA: Tax, TDS, stamp duty and loan-interest treatment depend on your facts. Confirm with a chartered accountant before you rely on any figure on this page.

Buying under construction in Whitefield, Sarjapur Road or along the ORR usually means your payments follow a plan while the building rises. A home loan for that purchase is rarely paid out in one shot. If you know how tranches and pre-EMI work, the later switch to full EMI won't catch you out.

You won't find interest rates, processing fees or loan-to-value percentages here. They change, and each bank sets its own. Your sanction letter is the source; ask your lender in writing if anything is unclear.

How is an under-construction home loan different from a ready-flat loan?

On a ready flat, the lender often disburses most or all of the loan at registration against a completed unit and clear documents. On an under-construction flat, the lender typically:

  1. Sanctions a maximum loan amount after its eligibility and project checks
  2. Disburses in tranches as construction milestones are certified
  3. Charges interest on the amount disbursed so far until full disbursement or EMI start, depending on the product

A builder's demand notice on its own often isn't enough. Many lenders want stage confirmation from their own panel, or from documents they accept.

What is pre-EMI?

In common borrower language, pre-EMI (or pre-EMI interest) is the interest you pay on the loan amount already disbursed, before the full EMI (principal + interest) begins. When full EMI starts (at full disbursement, on a fixed date, or at possession) is written into your loan agreement. A verbal promise from the sales team doesn't count. Ask the bank to point you to the clause.

Subvention or "no EMI till possession" marketing is a different structure and carries its own risks. Review those separately with your banker and CA; this page doesn't cover subvention schemes.

What documents do lenders usually ask for?

Lists vary. A typical under-construction file for a Bengaluru salaried buyer includes:

  • KYC and income proofs (salary slips, IT returns, bank statements)
  • Allotment letter and registered agreement for sale
  • Builder's demand notices and payment receipts
  • Project and promoter documents the bank's credit team already vetted for approved projects
  • Your contribution (margin) proof
  • Property papers required for creating the bank's charge

NRIs face extra attestation and account rules. Ask the bank's NRI desk for its current checklist instead of relying on a forwarded PDF.

How do tranches usually track construction?

A common pattern matches the payment plan in your agreement for sale: foundation, slab stages, brickwork, finishing, possession. Your lender may use its own stage list. Before you sign the agreement, ask:

  • Will my bank disburse to this builder and this project?
  • Which stages unlock which percentages of the sanctioned amount?
  • Who certifies the stage: the builder, an architect, or a bank-appointed engineer?
  • How many days after a demand does disbursement usually take?

The gap between a builder's demand and the bank's release squeezes many buyers' cash flow. Keep a buffer.

How does RERA connect to the loan file?

Lenders look for a registered project. Section 3 of the Act bars promoting or selling a project that needs registration before it is registered (s.3). Your agreement for sale should already respect section 13's advance limit (s.13). A construction-linked plan should match verifiable progress; our note on construction-linked vs time-linked plans explains why honest stage reporting matters. Before you sign, read what to check in the builder's sale agreement.

If the project is delayed beyond the agreement date, your section 18 rights against the promoter are separate from your duty to service the loan under the bank's agreement. Two contracts, two counterparties. Our note on who bears the interest when a project is delayed and EMIs are running walks through both.

What should I check in the sanction letter?

Read, at minimum:

  • Sanctioned amount and validity period
  • Rate type (fixed or floating) and when it resets
  • Pre-EMI vs EMI start trigger
  • Conditions precedent for first and later disbursements
  • Prepayment and foreclosure charges, if any
  • Insurance requirements
  • List of approved project stages

If anything is blank or "as per bank policy", ask for the policy extract in writing.

What happens if construction stalls after partial disbursement?

You may still owe pre-EMI on money already disbursed while the flat is unfinished. That's where builder delay and loan repayments collide. Track RERA quarterly updates (s.11(1)), keep the bank informed, and take legal advice early if possession looks unlikely by the agreement date. Don't stop loan payments because of a WhatsApp rumour. If things do go wrong, here is how to file a complaint with Karnataka RERA, and what happens to the loan if you cancel the booking and seek a refund.

Practical sequence for a Bengaluru under-construction buyer

  1. Get a provisional eligibility estimate from the lender before you lock a unit.
  2. Confirm the project is on the lender's approved list.
  3. Register the agreement for sale before large payments, per section 13.
  4. Pay your margin as the bank requires before tranche release.
  5. Diary each builder demand and bank disbursement.
  6. Revisit EMI affordability when full EMI start date is confirmed.

Why doesn't this page give a down-payment percentage?

There's no single minimum down payment, standard pre-EMI formula or Bengaluru-wide stage chart to quote. Those belong in RBI directions where applicable and in your bank's product sheet. If a consultant quotes a "usual" LTV as if it were law, ask them to show the circular.

Frequently asked questions

When does full EMI usually start on an under-construction loan?

When your loan agreement says it does, often at full disbursement or a linked date. Read the sanction letter and loan agreement rather than relying on sales talk.

Can I get a loan before the agreement for sale is registered?

Many lenders want the registered agreement before larger disbursements. Section 13 also limits how much the promoter can take before that agreement exists.

Who receives the tranche money?

Often the builder or a controlled payment path named in the bank's instructions, not your personal account. Confirm the payee with the bank for each tranche.

Is pre-EMI tax-deductible?

Treatment depends on the Income-tax Act provisions that apply to you and on whether the property is self-occupied or let. Ask a CA for the deduction that applies to you.

What if my builder demands money faster than the bank will disburse?

That cash-flow gap is common. Negotiate timing, keep a buffer, and don't breach section 13's advance limit to "keep the booking".
