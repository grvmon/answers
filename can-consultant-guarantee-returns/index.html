Can a consultant guarantee price appreciation or returns?

Nitin Gupta
Co-Founder · Investments & Decision Framework • Published: 18 Oct 2026 • 8 min read

Direct answer: No. A consultant can't guarantee that a flat will rise in value or earn a fixed rent, because neither depends on them. Section 10 of the RERA Act bars registered agents from misleading representations. Treat any "assured return" or "buyback" as a contract term: get it in writing from whoever promises it, and have your lawyer read it.

Verify with your lawyer: Whether a particular guarantee is enforceable, and against whom, depends on its exact wording and the parties who signed it. This page explains the RERA Act in general terms. It is not legal advice on your scheme or your agreement.

Most buyers hear some version of this line at least once: "This area will double in five years," or "The builder pays you rent until you move in." The first is a forecast. The second is a promise someone has to keep. Neither is a guarantee unless a party that can actually pay has signed it, and even then it is only as good as that party's finances.

Why can't anyone promise appreciation?

The value of a Bengaluru flat moves with things no consultant controls:

  • how many similar flats come up nearby over the next few years;
  • whether a planned metro line, road or flyover opens, and when;
  • home-loan interest rates and the wider economy;
  • employers moving into or out of the area;
  • how well the building and common areas are kept over time.

A good consultant studies these and gives you a view with reasons. A view is not a guarantee. It can be right on direction and wrong on timing for years, which is why your holding period and your ability to wait matter as much as the location. If someone gives you a single number for future value with no range and no reasons, ask what it is based on.

How do "assured return" offers usually work?

You will come across three common shapes.

Assured rental. The promoter, or a company linked to it, agrees to pay you a fixed monthly amount for a set period, often while it leases out the unit for you.

Buyback. The promoter agrees to buy the unit back at a stated price after a number of years, sometimes at your option, sometimes at theirs.

Subvention or "no EMI till possession". The promoter pays the interest on your home loan until a stated date. This is not a return, but it is often sold alongside one. Our page on how a home loan for an under-construction flat works explains the bank side of this.

None of these is automatically illegal. Each is only as good as:

  • the exact written terms;
  • who is promising (the promoter named on the RERA registration, a separate company, or just a salesperson);
  • that party's ability to pay when the time comes;
  • what you can do if they stop paying.

What does the RERA Act say about agents and promises?

Three sections matter most for a buyer.

Section 10 (agents). A registered real estate agent must not involve himself in unfair trade practices. The Act lists these as making any statement, orally, in writing or by visible representation, that falsely represents the services are of a particular standard, claims an approval or affiliation the promoter or agent does not have, or makes a false or misleading representation concerning the services. It also covers permitting an advertisement of services that are not intended to be offered. Section 62 lets the Authority penalise an agent who breaks section 9 or 10, up to ₹10,000 a day, capped at 5% of the cost of the unit whose sale the agent facilitated. Whether a specific promise about returns counts as misleading is for the Authority to decide on the facts.

Section 12 (promoter's advertisements). If you pay an advance or deposit relying on information in a notice, advertisement, prospectus or model apartment, and you suffer loss because a statement in it was incorrect or false, the promoter must compensate you. If you choose to withdraw from the project, the Act says you get your entire investment back with interest at the prescribed rate, plus compensation.

Section 13 (agreement for sale). A promoter cannot take more than 10% of the cost as an advance or application fee without first signing and registering a written agreement for sale. That agreement is where a real promise belongs. A guarantee that lives only in a brochure, a WhatsApp message or a separate side letter from another company is much harder to rely on.

If something goes wrong, section 31 lets you file a complaint with Karnataka RERA, and section 71 sends compensation claims to the adjudicating officer. What should I ask about any guarantee?

Ask Why it matters
Who exactly is legally promising this? A company other than the promoter may have few assets of its own
Is it written into the agreement for sale? Section 13 makes the registered agreement the main contract; a brochure line is weaker
What happens if payments stop? You want a stated remedy, not a verbal assurance
Is the "return" already built into the price? You may be prepaying your own rent
Can they end the scheme early, and on what notice? Exit clauses often sit in small print
What does my lawyer say? A reading from someone you appoint and pay

Ask for the scheme document before you pay any token. A checklist for the sale agreement helps you see whether the promise appears in the clauses or only in the marketing.

What can a consultant honestly offer instead?

  • A realistic range for rent, based on similar flats actually let nearby, with the comparables shown.
  • A base, good and bad case for value, each with its reasons.
  • A view on how easy the flat will be to resell.
  • The project and legal risks, stated plainly.
  • A straight "I don't know" when that is the honest answer.

Our page on what a real estate investment consultant does covers this in more detail. If a consultant pushes a guarantee hard, compare their pitch against these red flags when choosing a consultant.

Before you rely on any promise

  1. Find out who exactly is making it, by legal name.
  2. Get it written into the registered agreement, not only the brochure.
  3. Have your own lawyer read every clause about it.
  4. Ask what happens if it isn't honoured, and write the answer down.
  5. Decide whether you would still buy the flat without the promise. If not, think again.

Frequently asked questions

Are assured-return projects safe?

Some pay as promised and some stop. Judge the written terms and the financial strength of whoever signed the promise, not the headline percentage.

Is a buyback guarantee enforceable?

It depends on how it is worded and who is bound by it. A clause in the registered agreement with the promoter is easier to act on than a side letter. Ask your lawyer.

Can a consultant promise rent on my flat?

Not honestly. They can estimate a range from comparable flats let nearby. Ask to see those comparables.

What if an advertisement's promise turns out to be false?

Section 12 of the RERA Act says the promoter must compensate a buyer who paid relying on an incorrect or false statement in an advertisement or prospectus, and refund with interest if the buyer withdraws.

What if I have already signed up for an assured-return scheme?

Keep every document and payment record. If payments stop, speak to a lawyer, and consider a complaint to Karnataka RERA under section 31.
