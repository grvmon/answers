What hidden costs should I expect beyond the base price (GST, parking, PLC, maintenance, registration)?

Piyush Mittal
Partner & CBO · Partnerships & Deal Structuring • Published: 15 Oct 2026 • 10 min read
Share:

Direct answer: On top of the base price, a Bangalore flat usually carries some mix of floor rise and location charges, parking, clubhouse fees, GST on under-construction units, stamp duty and registration, utility deposits, advance maintenance, a corpus fund and legal fees. Amounts vary by project, so insist on a fully filled cost sheet and read it line by line.

Verify with your CA: Stamp duty, registration and tax figures here are examples. Confirm the numbers for your purchase with a chartered accountant before you pay.

Why is the base price misleading?

The base price is a marketing number. It's usually quoted per square foot on super built-up area, it leaves out most add-ons, and it doesn't include government charges. Two projects with the same base price can end up quite far apart once everything's added. The only number worth comparing is the total you'll pay to get the keys and move in.

We're not going to give you percentages or rupee ranges for these charges, because they vary by developer, project and unit and we'd rather not invent averages. What we can do is tell you every line to look for.

What goes on the cost sheet, line by line?

Charges set by the developer

Charge What it's What to ask
Floor rise Extra per sq ft for higher floors From which floor does it start, and at what rate?
PLC (preferential location charge) Premium for corner units, park or pool views, east-facing and so on Which units attract it on my shortlist?
Car park Covered, stilt, basement or open parking Is one included? What does a second cost? Is it allotted in writing?
Clubhouse / amenities One-time charge for club membership or amenity access Is it compulsory? When is the club due to be ready?
Advance maintenance Maintenance collected upfront, often for a year or more How many months, and at what rate per sq ft? Once the association bills monthly, check whether GST applies to maintenance.
Corpus or sinking fund One-time fund for future repairs, usually handed to the association Is it handed over to the association, and when? See corpus fund vs maintenance deposit.
Utility connection charges BESCOM, water and sewage connection costs passed on to you (how BWSSB water connection charges work; how permanent BESCOM power works) Are these actuals or a fixed estimate?
Generator / power backup Charge for backup capacity, sometimes per kVA How much backup is included?
Legal or documentation fee Developer's charge for preparing agreements What exactly does it cover?

None of these are wrong in themselves. What's wrong is finding out about them after you've booked.

Government charges

GST. Under the CGST Act, construction of a building intended for sale is treated as a supply of service, except where the entire consideration is received after the completion certificate is issued or after first occupation, whichever is earlier (Schedule II, paragraph 5(b)). In plain terms: on an under-construction flat, GST applies. On a completed flat where you pay everything after the completion certificate, it generally doesn't. The applicable rate depends on the unit's category, so get the GST line written on your cost sheet and check it with your CA rather than relying on a verbal figure. Our page on GST on a new flat: the 1% and 5% rates and when it is zero explains the categories.

Stamp duty and registration fee. These are paid when the sale deed (and, separately, the agreement for sale if registered) is registered at the Sub-Registrar's office. They're calculated with reference to the property value and the government guidance value. Rates are set by the state and do change, so check the current figures on the IGR Karnataka website or use the tools on Kaveri Online Services rather than trusting an old blog post. For how the agreement for sale and sale deed differ, see allotment letter vs agreement vs sale deed. For the registration day itself, see how to register a flat on Kaveri 2.0. For paying stamp duty online, see Karnataka Digital e-Stamp. Those charges don't change if the buyer is a woman. See stamp duty for women buyers in Karnataka.

TDS. This isn't an extra cost, but it catches people out. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, if you're buying immovable property worth ₹50 lakh or more from a resident seller, you deduct TDS from the payment and file it using Form 141 (Schedule B) within 30 days from the end of the month in which you deduct it. It comes out of what you pay the seller, not on top of it, but missing it can mean interest and fees. If the seller is a non-resident, a different part of Section 393 applies; see TDS when an NRI buys or sells a flat. Our page on TDS on property purchase from 1 April 2026 covers the rate, the ₹50 lakh test and Form 141. Your bank or CA can help.

Costs that aren't on any cost sheet

  • Home loan costs: processing fees, legal and valuation charges from the lender, and any insurance the bank asks for. On under-construction flats, also budget for pre-EMI; see how under-construction home-loan disbursement works. For how much of the price a bank can lend, see RBI's LTV and down payment rules.
  • Your own lawyer's fee for title and agreement review. Worth every rupee.
  • Interiors and fit-out: wardrobes, kitchen, lights, appliances. Often a big number for "bare shell" handovers.
  • Moving and setup: shifting, gas connection, internet.
  • Rent overlap if possession runs late and you're paying EMI and rent together. Still deciding whether to buy at all? See how to compare rent with the cost of owning in Bengaluru.

How do you read a cost sheet without getting lost?

  1. Ask for it per unit, not per project. Floor rise and PLC differ unit by unit. Here is what RERA says about floor-rise and PLC.
  2. Check the area basis. Section 4(2)(h) of the RERA Act requires the promoter to disclose the carpet area of apartments when registering. If the cost sheet is in super built-up area, ask for the carpet area too, and compare price per carpet sq ft across projects. Our page on carpet area vs super built-up area under RERA shows the arithmetic.
  3. Look for "as applicable" or "extra at actuals." Ask for an estimate in writing for every such line.
  4. Check payment timing. When is each add-on due? Some are collected at possession, which affects your cash planning.
  5. Compare totals, not base prices. Put two or three projects in one spreadsheet with the same columns.

Which cost lines do buyers most often miss?

Missed item Why it's missed How to catch it
Second parking slot Assumed to be included Ask how many slots are allotted, in writing
Maintenance advance Shown at possession stage, not on the booking sheet Ask for the possession-stage demand list
Corpus fund Labelled differently by each developer Ask "is there any one-time fund collected at handover?"
Utility deposits Called "charges at actuals" Ask for last project's actuals as a guide
Interest on delayed payments Hidden in the agreement Read the payment default clause in the draft agreement

What should you do before you compare projects?

  1. Get a filled cost sheet for the specific unit you're considering.
  2. Ask in writing for every "as applicable" line to be estimated.
  3. Confirm the GST treatment based on the project's stage, and check it with a CA.
  4. Check current stamp duty and registration rates on the IGR Karnataka website.
  5. Add your own costs: lawyer, loan charges, interiors, moving.
  6. Compare total cost per carpet sq ft across your shortlist.
  7. Read the payment default and escalation clauses in the draft agreement (see whether a builder can raise the price after booking).

Frequently asked questions

Is GST payable on a ready-to-move flat in Bangalore?

Generally not, if the entire price is paid after the completion certificate is issued (or after first occupation, whichever is earlier). If any part was paid before that, GST can apply. Check your specific case with a CA.

Can I negotiate parking, PLC or clubhouse charges?

Sometimes. Developers handle these differently, and launch phases or slow-moving units can have more room. Ask for any waiver in writing on the cost sheet.

Who pays stamp duty and registration on a new flat?

Usually the buyer, as part of registering the sale deed. Check what your agreement says, and check current rates on the IGR Karnataka site.

Is maintenance included in the flat price?

Usually not. Most developers collect advance maintenance separately, often at possession. Ask how many months and at what rate.

How can a consultant help with hidden costs?

By asking for every line in writing, comparing projects on the same basis, and pointing out charges you hadn't thought to ask about. They can't tell you current tax rates with authority; check those with official sources or a CA.
