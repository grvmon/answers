How do property consultants in Bangalore get paid, by the buyer or the developer?

By acreNkey Advisory Team · Updated 14 Oct 2026

Direct answer: On primary sales, a property consultant Bangalore homebuyers meet is usually paid by the developer when a booking happens through them, not via a separate buyer invoice. Buyers often pay no advisory fee at all. That doesn't make the advice unbiased, so always ask who pays. acreNkey’s advice is free to the buyer; we're an authorised sales partner for some projects and may be paid by the developer.

Property consultant Bangalore pay models: ask about money first

Free to you isn't the same as independent of the seller. That's the one line to carry away from this page.

Bangalore homebuyers hear “no brokerage from your side” and relax. But the service still runs on a commercial engine. On new-project sales, that engine is usually a developer-paid channel payout. On some resale deals, buyer-side or seller-side brokerage models appear instead. Percentages and slabs vary by developer, project and relationship. We aren't publishing a commission figure here, because there's no single public tariff.

What to remember

  • On new launches the developer often pays; on resale the seller often does. It varies.
  • “Free for you” describes your invoice, not the consultant's incentives.
  • Ask who pays on your project, and get it in writing.

The main payment models you'll meet

1) Developer-paid channel / sales partner (very common on primary)

  • You book a new apartment.
  • The developer (or its sales network) pays the consultant / channel partner as per their internal commercial terms.
  • You may not see that payout on your cost sheet.
  • Your risk: the advice can tilt toward inventory that pays the consultant rather than the home that fits you best.

2) Buyer-paid advisory fee (less common in mass primary sales)

  • You pay a fee for shortlisting, due diligence coordination or search work.
  • This can reduce developer-pay bias, but only if the consultant isn't also collecting developer payouts on the same deal without telling you.
  • Ask whether any developer incentive still applies on top.

3) Seller-paid brokerage (more typical in resale)

  • The seller pays a brokerage to the agent who closes the resale.
  • You can still get steered if the agent’s easiest deal sits with a talkative seller rather than the right home.

4) Mixed or unclear

  • “I am free for you” plus silence about the developer side.
  • Side retainers, soft benefits, or launch contests.
  • Treat opacity as a problem, not as a free gift.

What Indian RERA does and doesn't fix about fees

RERA is strict on registration and conduct for real estate agents. Section 9 requires registration for facilitating sale or purchase in registered projects. Section 10 bans unfair trade practices and requires agents to help allottees get information they're entitled to at booking.

RERA does not magically erase commercial incentives. A registered agent can still be developer-paid. Registration answers “are they allowed to facilitate?” It doesn't answer “are their incentives aligned with my brief?”

If someone hides behind RERA language to dodge a fee conversation (“We're RERA, so we're neutral”), that's a category error. Neutral is a behaviour, not a certificate.

How to ask without making it awkward

Keep it short and direct:

  • “On this project, who pays you if I book?”
  • “Are you an authorised sales partner here?”
  • “Will you show me options where you earn nothing if those fit my brief better?”
  • “Please put the fee arrangement in one email sentence.”

You aren't being rude. You're doing basic consumer hygiene on a multi-lakh or crore decision.

Myths Bangalore buyers should drop

Myth: “If I am not paying, I am the client.”
Reality: The payer often shapes urgency and inventory. You can still be respected as the decision-maker, but you should verify that with behaviour.

Myth: “Builder desk is always cheaper than going through a consultant.”
Reality: Commercial terms vary. Sometimes channel inventory and desks differ; sometimes they don't. Compare written cost sheets, not folklore.

Myth: “NRI buyers must pay extra advisory fees to be safe.”
Reality: Remote process quality matters. An extra fee can be worth it if scope is clear. It isn't mandatory just because you're abroad.

Myth: “Commission percentages are standardised across Whitefield and Sarjapur Road.”
Reality: They vary. Anyone quoting a city-wide fixed percentage as gospel is simplifying for effect.

Practical bias controls (even when the developer pays)

  1. Freeze your brief in writing before the first site visit.
  2. Ask for at least one rejected option with reasons.
  3. Compare two projects on the same corridor using the same cost-sheet template.
  4. Separate “fit for my life” from “hot launch this week”.
  5. Keep lawyer review outside the sales chain.
  6. Re-ask the payment question if the shortlist suddenly becomes one developer only.

Frequently asked questions

Do buyers in Bangalore normally pay property consultants on new flats?

Often they don't pay a separate advisory invoice on primary sales, because developers commonly pay channel partners. That pattern is common, not universal. Ask for your case.

Can a consultant be paid by both buyer and developer on the same deal?

It can happen in messy arrangements. If both sides might pay, you want that stated before you proceed. Unclear double compensation is a trust failure.

Will developer payment force them to hide problems?

Not necessarily, but it can soften pushback. Judge consultants by whether they raise RERA gaps, possession risk and cost add-ons even on partnered projects.

Are commission rates public on Karnataka RERA?

Agent registration and project particulars are what the public portal is built to show. Developer-channel commercial payouts are typically private contracts, not a buyer-facing tariff board.
