What happens to my flat if the builder goes insolvent?

Piyush Mittal
Partner & CBO · Partnerships & Deal Structuring • Published: 18 Oct 2026 • 9 min read

Direct answer: If a real estate developer becomes insolvent, your flat is not automatically cancelled or transferred to another buyer. However, possession can be delayed, the project may be restructured, and recovering money paid becomes more complex. Under Section 5(8)(f) of the IBC, qualifying buyers are financial creditors who participate in the Committee of Creditors through an authorised representative. Neither possession nor an immediate refund is guaranteed; outcomes depend on the approved resolution plan or statutory liquidation distribution.

Verify with your lawyer: This page explains the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (including amendments up to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026) and the RERA Act as published. Have your own property and insolvency lawyer evaluate your specific agreement, corporate debtor identity, and ongoing tribunal filings before you submit, vote, or settle anything.

If a real estate developer becomes insolvent, your flat is not automatically cancelled or transferred to another buyer. However, possession can be delayed, the project may be restructured, and recovering the money you paid can become more complicated.

Your rights depend on the legal entity that entered insolvency, the status of the project, the terms of your agreement, and the resolution process approved by the authorities.

1. What does builder insolvency mean?

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) provides a legal process for resolving the financial distress of eligible companies and other corporate entities.

A builder missing possession deadlines, defaulting on payments or facing financial difficulties does not, by itself, mean that insolvency proceedings have begun. Corporate insolvency resolution proceedings generally begin when the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) admits an application under the IBC.

First, establish which legal entity entered insolvency. A developer's brand name, parent company, project company and the entity named in your agreement may be different. The identity of the corporate debtor matters when determining which assets, liabilities and proceedings are involved.

2. Are homebuyers recognised as financial creditors?

Yes. Under Section 5(8)(f) of the IBC, qualifying amounts raised from allottees under real estate projects are recognised as financial debt. Eligible homebuyers are therefore financial creditors.

This status can allow homebuyers to submit claims in an ongoing corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) and participate in the Committee of Creditors through the prescribed authorised-representative mechanism.

However, recognition as a financial creditor does not guarantee that every claim will be admitted in full or that the buyer will receive an immediate refund.

3. Can one homebuyer initiate insolvency proceedings against a builder?

Generally, an individual homebuyer cannot initiate CIRP against a corporate developer solely on the strength of their own allotment.

The second proviso to Section 7(1) requires an application by homebuyers to be filed jointly by:

  • At least 100 allottees in the same real estate project; or
  • At least 10% of the total number of allottees in that project,

whichever number is lower.

For example, if a project has 80 allottees, the 10% threshold is 8. If it has 1,000 allottees, the 10% threshold is 100.

The statutory requirement concerns the number of qualifying allottees in the same project. The project's legal structure and the facts of the proposed application must be checked before calculating the applicable threshold.

Important distinction: This threshold applies to homebuyers seeking to initiate CIRP. It does not mean that every homebuyer must join the initiating group to submit a claim in an already-admitted insolvency proceeding.

4. What happens after the NCLT admits insolvency proceedings?

Once CIRP is admitted, a moratorium under Section 14 generally restricts specified proceedings and enforcement actions against the corporate debtor, subject to the Code and applicable exceptions.

An interim resolution professional or resolution professional takes charge of the process in accordance with the IBC. Creditors submit their claims for verification, and the insolvency process evaluates possible resolutions.

As a homebuyer, you should:

  1. Locate the public announcement and identify the appointed insolvency professional.
  2. Submit your claim using the prescribed form and supporting documents within the applicable deadline.
  3. Preserve your allotment letter, builder-buyer agreement, payment receipts, demand letters, possession commitments and relevant correspondence.
  4. Track claim verification, Committee of Creditors decisions and proposed resolution plans.
  5. Obtain legal advice before withdrawing or settling a claim, or taking action that could affect your position in the process.

Do not assume that a complaint, demand letter or case filed before insolvency will automatically be treated as a submitted and admitted claim.

5. Will you receive possession or a refund?

Neither outcome is guaranteed.

Depending on the facts and the approved resolution plan, the project may be completed by the existing corporate debtor, transferred to another resolution applicant, restructured, or dealt with through an arrangement involving the sale of assets. The terms of the approved plan determine the treatment of affected stakeholders, subject to applicable law.

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 amended the statutory definition of a resolution plan to expressly contemplate the sale of one or more assets through one or more plans proposed by resolution applicants, subject to specified conditions.

This flexibility may create different routes for resolving assets belonging to a distressed developer. It should not be interpreted as a guarantee that every housing project will be separated, transferred or completed independently.

If the corporate debtor enters liquidation rather than achieving a successful resolution, creditor recoveries are governed by the applicable statutory distribution framework. Recovery may be less than the amount claimed.

6. Can a homebuyers' association represent everyone?

An association can help coordinate communication and collective action, but it should not be assumed that an RWA or housing society automatically acquires the legal standing of every individual homebuyer.

In Elegna Co-operative Housing and Commercial Society Ltd. v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd., decided on 15 January 2026, the Supreme Court addressed whether a homebuyers' society could intervene in insolvency proceedings merely by representing its members. The judgment reinforces the importance of using the statutory routes available to eligible creditors and authorised representatives.

Homebuyers should establish whether claims must be submitted individually, how they will be represented in the Committee of Creditors, and whether the association has an independent legal basis to act in the specific proceeding.

7. Can you still use RERA or other legal remedies?

Potentially, yes. The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA) and the IBC operate in different legal spheres, and a homebuyer may have rights under more than one applicable law.

However, the interaction between insolvency proceedings and other remedies depends on the nature of the claim, the relief sought, the entity involved and the stage of CIRP. A moratorium or an approved resolution plan may affect how particular proceedings or claims can continue.

Do not assume that every parallel case can continue unchanged, or that filing a RERA complaint will necessarily deliver a refund or possession when the developer is insolvent.

Seek advice from a lawyer familiar with both insolvency and real estate regulation before deciding which remedy to pursue.

8. Does the law distinguish genuine homebuyers from speculative investors?

The courts examine the facts of the transaction. The Supreme Court's September 2025 decision in Mansi Brar Fernandes v. Shubha Sharma discusses the distinction between a genuine allottee and a speculative investor, including the terms of buy-back or refund arrangements and the allottee's overall conduct.

A special arrangement offering guaranteed returns, coupled with an absence of genuine interest in obtaining possession, can invite scrutiny. However, no single factor should be presented as a universal test without reference to the applicable judgment and facts.

Homebuyers should retain the complete transaction record and obtain advice on their particular circumstances.

9. What should you do if your builder is facing insolvency?

Begin by verifying whether insolvency proceedings have actually been admitted. A financial problem or delayed project is not proof that CIRP is underway.

If a case has been admitted, identify the correct corporate debtor, locate the resolution professional's public announcement, submit your claim and retain proof of submission. Review the proposed resolution plan carefully before deciding whether to support or challenge a course of action.

For a large investment, seek advice on the claim amount, contractual rights, limitation periods, applicable moratorium and the consequences of any settlement.

The key takeaway: Insolvency law provides a framework for resolving a distressed developer's affairs; it does not guarantee that every homebuyer will receive a refund or possession. Understanding the legal entity, filing a properly documented claim and monitoring the approved resolution process are essential.

Frequently asked questions

Are homebuyers recognised as financial creditors under the IBC?

Yes. Under Section 5(8)(f) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, qualifying amounts raised from allottees under real estate projects are recognised as financial debt, making eligible homebuyers financial creditors.

Can one homebuyer initiate insolvency proceedings against a builder?

No. Under the second proviso to Section 7(1), an application by homebuyers must be filed jointly by at least 100 allottees or at least 10% of total allottees in the same real estate project, whichever is lower.

Can I continue my RERA complaint after the builder enters insolvency?

Section 14 imposes a moratorium prohibiting institution or continuation of proceedings against the corporate debtor before any court, tribunal or authority. However, RERA and IBC operate under distinct legal spheres; consult legal counsel on whether and how particular parallel remedies interact.

Does builder insolvency guarantee possession or a refund?

Neither outcome is guaranteed. The outcome depends entirely on the resolution plan approved by the Committee of Creditors and NCLT, or statutory distribution priority under liquidation if no plan succeeds.

Can a homebuyers' association automatically represent every buyer?

Not automatically. In Elegna Co-operative Housing (Jan 2026), the Supreme Court clarified that associations do not automatically replace individual statutory creditor standing. Buyers should file individual claims and participate through the authorised representative.
