Living along the ORR: what do noise and air quality data say before you buy?

Gaurav Mongia
Co-Founder · Market Strategy, Product & Tech • Published: 17 Oct 2026 • 7 min read
Share:

Direct answer: If you're comparing Bellandur flats for sale on the outer ring road, test noise and air for the specific flat. The legal residential noise limit is 55 dB(A) by day and 45 at night. The nearest official air monitor, at Kadabesanahalli, averaged about 37 µg/m³ of PM2.5 in one 2019–20 study, near the 40 annual limit.

What are the key takeaways?

  • Noise limits are set by law: 55 dB(A) day and 45 dB(A) night for residential areas, 65 and 55 for commercial.
  • There's an official air monitor on the ORR belt: CPCB's BWSSB Kadabesanahalli station, near Panathur.
  • One study found it higher than residential sites: about 37 µg/m³ of PM2.5 against about 26 µg/m³ at kerbside and residential sites, July 2019 to June 2020.
  • Distance helps with road noise, but only so much; a rough rule is 3 dB less per doubling of distance from a busy road.
  • Our HEAR check: height and facing, exposure distance, air data, return at night.

The ORR between Marathahalli and Bellandur is lined with tech parks, flyovers and, now, Blue Line metro construction. Apartments sit right beside it. That's convenient for work. It's also loud and busy. This page shows what the official numbers say and how to check one flat, because two flats in the same project can sound very different.

What are the legal noise limits?

The Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 set ambient limits by zone, measured as dB(A) Leq, a time-weighted average (CPCB):

Zone Day (6 am–10 pm) Night (10 pm–6 am)
Industrial 75 70
Commercial 65 55
Residential 55 45
Silence zone 50 40

The state decides which zone an area falls in. A stretch of ORR lined with offices and shops may be treated as commercial, not residential. We couldn't find a published zone map or public noise readings for this stretch, so the limits tell you the target, not what you'll hear.

What does the official air data show?

CPCB lists 14 continuous air monitoring stations in Bengaluru, including BWSSB Kadabesanahalli, Silk Board and BTM Layout (CPCB station list). Kadabesanahalli is the one on the ORR belt near Panathur.

A peer-reviewed CSTEP study compared regulatory stations from July 2019 to June 2020. Kerbside and residential sites averaged about 26 µg/m³ of PM2.5, while the two sites it classed as industrial, Peenya and BWSSB, averaged about 37 µg/m³ (CSTEP study, PMC). That period included the 2020 lockdown, so treat it as indicative, not current. The national annual standard for PM2.5 is 40 µg/m³ (CPCB NAAQS).

Worked example 1: how close to the limit?

Site group PM2.5 average (2019–20) Share of the 40 µg/m³ annual limit
Kerbside and residential stations ~26 µg/m³ 26 ÷ 40 = 65%
BWSSB and Peenya (industrial) ~37 µg/m³ 37 ÷ 40 = 93%

One station can't tell you about one flat a kilometre away. It does tell you this belt isn't among the cleaner parts of the city in that study. You can see current readings for Kadabesanahalli on CPCB's live dashboard.

How much does distance from the road help?

For a busy road, a common acoustic rule of thumb is that noise falls by about 3 dB each time you double your distance from it, in open ground with nothing in between. Buildings, walls and trees change that a lot, so use it only to compare.

Worked example 2: front row or set back?

Flat's distance from the ORR Doublings from 25 m Rough reduction
25 m (front tower) 0 0 dB
50 m 1 about 3 dB
100 m 2 about 6 dB
200 m (rear tower) 3 about 9 dB

A 10 dB drop is often described as sounding about half as loud. So a rear tower can be noticeably quieter than the front one in the same project, especially if another tower blocks the road. Facing matters too: a bedroom on the far side of the building is shielded by the building itself.

How does the HEAR check work?

  1. Height and facing. Which way do the bedrooms face? Is there a flyover at the same level as your floor? Our guide to floor choice and corner units covers the trade-offs.
  2. Exposure distance. How far is the flat from the ORR, a flyover or the metro line? Measure on a map.
  3. Air data. Check the nearest CPCB station's recent readings, especially in winter when levels tend to be higher.
  4. Return at night. Visit after 10 pm and early morning. Stand in the bedroom with the windows shut, then open.

What should you check before buying beside the ORR?

  • Bedroom facing and floor checked against the road and any flyover
  • Distance to the ORR and metro viaduct measured
  • Night visit after 10 pm and an early-morning visit done
  • Windows' glazing type asked about
  • Kadabesanahalli or nearest station readings checked
  • Residents asked about construction and traffic noise
  • A rear or side unit compared, if the project has one

Frequently asked questions

What is the legal noise limit for a residential area in India?

55 dB(A) during the day (6 am to 10 pm) and 45 dB(A) at night, under the Noise Pollution Rules, 2000. Commercial areas are 65 and 55. The state decides which zone an area falls in.

Is air quality bad along the ORR in Bellandur?

In a July 2019 to June 2020 study, the official BWSSB Kadabesanahalli station averaged about 37 µg/m³ of PM2.5, higher than residential sites at about 26 and close to the 40 annual limit. Check current readings on CPCB's dashboard.

Where is the nearest official air monitor to Bellandur and Marathahalli?

CPCB's BWSSB Kadabesanahalli station, on the ORR belt near Panathur. Silk Board and BTM Layout also have stations.

Are rear towers quieter than front towers?

Usually. Road noise falls by roughly 3 dB per doubling of distance in open ground, and another building in between blocks more. Visit both at night to hear the difference.

Does the Blue Line construction add noise?

Construction along the ORR is noisy while it lasts. Ask residents about night work and check the line's expected completion on our metro page.

What is the HEAR check?

It's our four-part check for noise and air: height and facing of the flat, exposure distance from the road, air data from the nearest official station, and returning at night to listen.
