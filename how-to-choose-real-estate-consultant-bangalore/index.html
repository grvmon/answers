How do I choose a trustworthy real estate consultant in Bangalore?

Piyush Mittal Partner & CBO
Last modified: 24 Oct 2026 • 8 min read
Share:

Direct answer: To choose a real estate consultant in Bangalore you can trust, verify their Karnataka RERA agent registration, ask who pays them, and see whether they’ll say “no” to a bad fit. Trustworthy consultants explain their fee arrangement in plain words and give you time to read documents before any token.

Which trust tests should a real estate consultant in Bangalore pass?

Most buyers start with a friend’s forward or a portal lead. That's a fine way to find names and a weak way to judge trust. Run these five checks before you share your budget or PAN.

  1. Registration: Can they give a Karnataka RERA agent registration number you can verify yourself?
  2. Payment disclosure: Will they say, without hedging, whether a developer pays them on the projects they show?
  3. Corridor depth: Can they talk you through Whitefield versus Sarjapur Road versus Varthur trade-offs without reading from a PDF?
  4. Refusal muscle: Will they tell you a project is a poor fit for you?
  5. Process pace: Do they protect time for RERA and paperwork checks, or push same-day tokens?

If someone fails two of these five, keep looking.

The short version

  • Registration is the floor, not the ceiling.
  • Payment disclosure in one written line beats any amount of charm.
  • A consultant who never says “not for you” isn't filtering.

What red flags show up early in Bangalore?

  • They can't explain the difference between project RERA registration and their own agent registration.
  • They discourage you from speaking to a lawyer until after the token.
  • Every “favoured” project happens to be a launch with heavy channel payouts, and they never show ready inventory even when your brief asks for it.
  • They ask for a “processing fee” or “token to me personally” outside the developer’s official collection process. (Payment routes vary; treat unofficial personal collections as a hard stop until explained in writing by the developer.)
  • They mock RERA as “just a formality”. Section 9 of the RERA Act, 2016 makes agent registration a legal requirement for facilitating sale or purchase in registered projects. Section 10 bars facilitating unregistered projects and unfair trade practices.

What should I ask on the first call?

Copy these into your notes app:

  1. What is your Karnataka RERA agent registration number, and is it under your name or a firm?
  2. On the projects you'll show me this month, who pays you if I book?
  3. Which East Bangalore corridors do you actually cover week to week?
  4. Tell me about a recent buyer brief you declined to service, and why.
  5. What documents do you want me to see before any token?
  6. If I want a second opinion from a lawyer, will you share the full document pack without delay?

Listen to how they answer as much as what they say. Vague charm costs nothing; clear sentences take work.

Why does East Bangalore specificity matter?

A consultant who mostly works South or North Bengaluru can be perfectly honest and still miss East-specific traps: half-built social infrastructure near Gunjur or Budigere Cross, ITPL peak-hour choke, Sarjapur Road stretch variability, or civic jurisdiction differences as you move from Mahadevapura towards Hoskote. If you're a family buying to live in, or an NRI who can't fly in often, that local pattern recognition matters as much as the amenity list.

Searching mostly in Whitefield? Ask for Whitefield examples. Sarjapur Road? Ask about that corridor. Generic “Bangalore expert” branding isn't enough.

Buyer checklist: what to do next

  1. Score at least two consultants on the scorecard. With only one, you've nothing to compare against.
  2. Check each RERA agent number yourself on the Karnataka RERA portal (how to verify RERA registration walks you through it).
  3. Get the fee arrangement in one written line: who pays them, and on which project types.
  4. Ask for a corridor-specific shortlist built around your own commute anchors (ITPL, ORR, Whitefield, Sarjapur Road, and so on).
  5. Don't pay a token until you've worked through documents to verify before token.
  6. Keep one advisor, your lawyer, outside the sales chain.

Does firm size matter?

A large firm may have more listings and more staff. A small firm may give you more of the founder's time. Neither is automatically better. What matters is the person handling your case and how thorough they're. Size alone isn't the filter; score the person and the process. For the person-level checklist, see how to choose a real estate consultant in Bangalore.

How does a sample-work test help?

Pick one project you're actually considering. Ask each shortlisted firm for a one-page view: what's good, what worries them, what they'd check next. Compare the pages side by side.

What to compare Good sign
RERA details Registration number, declared completion date, approvals mentioned
Cost All-in, not just base price
Risks At least two specific concerns
Disclosure Whether they're paid on this project
Next steps Clear checks to do before paying

The firm that's honest about a project's problems, even one it's paid on, is usually the one to pick. Don't mark a firm down for saying "I'd need to see the title papers before I can comment." That's the right answer.

What can you do if a firm misleads you?

Choosing well includes knowing your fallback. A consultancy that facilitates sales in registered projects is a real estate agent under the Act, and section 10 sets out what it must not do. It can't facilitate the sale of a unit in an unregistered project, and it can't make false statements about its services, claim approval or affiliation it doesn't have, or use other unfair practices. Section 10 also obliges it to help you get the information and documents you're entitled to at the time of booking.

If a firm breaks those rules, section 31 lets you complain to Karnataka RERA, and section 62 sets a penalty for agents who breach sections 9 or 10 of up to ₹10,000 for each day the default continues, cumulatively up to five per cent of the unit's cost. You won't need any of this with a good firm. Asking a firm how it handles complaints, and watching how it answers, is still one of the better tests you can run.

Frequently asked questions

Is a RERA-registered agent automatically trustworthy?

No. Registration is a baseline legal filter, not a character certificate. You still need disclosure, local depth and document discipline.

Should I prefer a big national brokerage over a local consultant?

Size can mean process and inventory access. It can also mean you're only a lead ID. For East Bangalore self-use buys, a smaller team with corridor depth and clear disclosure often serves you better than a national logo with a rotating caller.

What if a consultant refuses to put fee disclosure in writing?

Treat that as a failed trust test. If they can't write one sentence about who pays them, they're asking you to guess their incentives.

Can I work with two consultants at once?

Yes, early on. Be transparent if both are arranging visits to the same project, so site teams aren't played against each other in a messy way. Once you shortlist seriously, pick one primary coordinator.

Does “free for buyers” mean neutral?

Not by itself. Free to you can still mean paid by a developer. Free is about your invoice, not about incentive design. See how consultants get paid.

What if a consultant refuses to answer about payment?

Treat that as a no. You're making one of the biggest purchases of your life; you're entitled to know their incentives.
