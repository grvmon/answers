Real estate consultant vs broker vs advisory firm: which should a Bangalore buyer use?

By Gaurav Mongia, acre&key · Published 15 Oct 2026 · Updated 15 Oct 2026

Direct answer: Use a broker when you mainly need listings and someone to close. Use a consultant, usually developer-paid, for help shortlisting and checking new projects. Use a fee-paid advisory firm when you want someone paid only by you. The labels overlap a lot in Bangalore, so judge people by who pays them and what they'll do.

Disclosure: acre&key’s advice is free to buyers; we are an authorised sales partner for some projects and may be paid by the developer.

Start with this: the law doesn't care about the label

Under section 2(zm) of the RERA Act, a "real estate agent" is anyone who negotiates or acts for someone in a sale of a unit in a real estate project and receives remuneration or fees, whether as commission or otherwise. It also covers anyone who introduces prospective buyers and sellers to each other. Section 9 requires them to register with the state authority, and section 10 sets their duties.

So "broker," "consultant," "channel partner" and many "advisors" can all be real estate agents in the legal sense when they're facilitating a sale in a registered project. The difference that matters to you is the business model: who pays them, and what work they take on.

How do the three models compare?

Broker Consultant (often a channel or sales partner) Real estate advisory (fee-paid, buyer-side)
Typical focus Resale and rentals, some new projects New projects, sometimes resale Your whole decision, any stock
Who usually pays Seller, buyer or both, by local custom Developer, on partnered projects You
Main strength Inventory access, local deal flow Shortlisting, launch access, process help No seller money in your deal
Main risk Pushing whatever's available Steering to partnered projects Cost, and checking they really don't take seller money
Paperwork help Varies widely Usually coordinates checks Usually deeper review, still not a lawyer
Stays after booking? Often not Varies Usually, if agreed

These are typical patterns, not rules. Plenty of brokers are careful and plenty of advisors are sloppy. The table tells you what to ask about, not who to trust.

Which one fits your situation?

You're buying a resale flat in a specific society. A local broker who knows that society may be the quickest route. Ask how their brokerage works and who pays it. Add a lawyer for the title. For the extra checks, see buying a resale flat.

You're buying a new flat and comparing several projects. A consultant who covers your corridor can save you a lot of legwork, as long as they disclose which projects pay them. This is where most East Bengaluru first-time buyers land.

You want advice from someone with no seller money involved. A fee-only real estate advisory makes sense, especially for complex or high-value purchases. Make sure the agreement says they won't take developer or seller payouts on your deal.

You're an NRI buying remotely. What matters most is process: video visits, document handling, someone on the ground for registration. Any of the three can work if they've done remote purchases before. More in buying a Bangalore flat as an NRI without travelling.

What questions cut through the labels?

Whatever they call themselves, ask:

  1. "Who pays you if I buy through you, and on which projects?"
  2. "What's your Karnataka RERA agent registration number?"
  3. "What will you do after I book?"
  4. "Which parts of the paperwork do you check, and which should go to my lawyer?"
  5. "When did you last tell a client not to buy something?"

The answers will tell you which model they really work under.

Can one firm be more than one of these?

Yes, and many are. A consultancy might be a sales partner on some projects, take brokerage on resale, and charge a fee for a detailed advisory report. That's fine if each arrangement is clear on your deal. The thing to watch for is someone claiming to be fee-paid only while also collecting from the developer on the unit you're buying. Section 10(c) of the RERA Act bars agents from making false or misleading representations about their services, and that kind of claim can fall into it. See can the same firm be a channel partner and your advisor?

So which should you pick?

  • If keeping seller money out of it matters most and can pay for it: fee-paid advisory.
  • If you want help comparing new projects without a fee and can live with disclosed developer pay: consultant.
  • If you need access to a specific resale or rental listing: broker.
  • In every case: verify RERA registration, get the payment arrangement in writing, and keep your own lawyer.

What does advisory work actually look like?

  • Understanding your goals: home, investment, both; timeline; budget.
  • Comparing locations on commute, schools, infrastructure that's actually running, and resale.
  • Comparing projects on carpet-area pricing, approvals, builder record and payment plans.
  • Reading documents: RERA pages, title papers, the draft agreement.
  • Running numbers: total cost, EMIs, rent if investing.
  • Flagging risks.
  • Telling you when not to buy.

How do you tell real advice from a sales pitch?

Starts with your goals Starts with a project
Shows options from several builders Shows one or two

Says who pays them Avoids it
Is fine with "wait" Pushes urgency

Is a consultant automatically better than a broker?

It's easy to assume "consultant" means more expert. Not always. What matters is whether the person:

  • tells you who pays them;
  • is properly registered;
  • knows your area in detail;
  • is willing to say "don't buy this";
  • puts things in writing.

Someone who does all five is worth working with, whatever they call themselves.

Your next steps

  1. Decide what you actually need help with: finding stock, comparing projects, or judgement from someone only you pay.
  2. Pick the model that matches, using the table above.
  3. Talk to two people in that model and ask the five questions.
  4. Verify their agent registration on rera.karnataka.gov.in.
  5. Get who-pays-whom in one written line before the first site visit.

Frequently asked questions

Is a real estate advisory firm always unbiased?

No. Fee-paid advisory removes one source of bias, but you should still confirm in writing that they take no developer or seller money on your deal.

Do brokers in Bangalore need RERA registration?

If they're facilitating the sale or purchase of a unit in a RERA-registered project, section 9 requires registration. Resale and rental work outside registered projects may sit differently; ask a lawyer about specific cases.

Is a channel partner the same as a consultant?

Often, yes. "Channel partner" usually means a consultant or firm with a commercial tie-up with a developer. It's a description of who pays them, not a separate legal category.

Which option is cheapest for me?

A developer-paid consultant usually costs you nothing directly. A fee-paid advisor costs money. A broker's cost depends on local practice and the deal. Cheapest isn't always best value, so weigh whose interests the fee buys.

Is an adviser legally required to act in my interest?

There's no specific Indian law making property advisers fiduciaries in the way some financial advisers are. Your protection comes from the RERA Act's rules for agents and whatever your agreement says.
