How do I check whether an East Bangalore project falls under BBMP or a gram panchayat?

Arvind Patidar
Co-Founder · Operations, CEX & Tech • Published: 15 Oct 2026 • 8 min read
Share:

Direct answer: Check who issues the khata and collects property tax on the project land, and who sanctioned the building plan. A khata from the city corporation (the former BBMP area, now Greater Bengaluru Authority) through e-Aasthi means a city property; one from a gram panchayat through e-Swathu means panchayat. Confirm it from documents on the project's Karnataka RERA page.

Verify with your CA: Tax treatment depends on your income, tax regime and facts. Check with a chartered accountant before you file, deduct or pay.

Verify with your lawyer: This is general information, not legal advice. Have a property lawyer check your documents before you sign or pay.

What changed with the name?

You'll still hear "BBMP" everywhere, and many documents carry it. Since 2025, Bengaluru's civic administration has been reorganised under the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA), with five city corporations below it. The official site still sits at bbmp.gov.in and now carries the GBA name. For a buyer, the practical question is the same as before: is this land inside the city corporation area, or is it governed by a gram panchayat?

Boundaries can change over time, so always check current records for the specific survey number rather than relying on what was true a few years ago.

Why does it matter on the East side?

East Bengaluru's growth has pushed well past the old city limits. Parts of Varthur, Gunjur, Sarjapur Road's outer stretches, Budigere Cross, Hoskote and the Old Madras Road fringe include land that's under gram panchayats, while nearby pockets are inside the city corporation area. Two projects a kilometre apart can sit under different authorities, which is one more thing to weigh in Whitefield vs Sarjapur Road.

That difference affects:

  • Khata and property tax. City properties are managed through GBA's e-Aasthi system. Panchayat properties are recorded in e-Swathu.
  • Home loans. Lenders look closely at khata type and plan approvals.
  • Civic services. Water supply, drainage and road maintenance arrangements differ.
  • Plan approvals. The authority that sanctioned the building plan differs, and it's worth knowing which one did.

None of this makes panchayat projects bad. It just changes what you need to check.

Which five checks settle it?

1. Read the project's RERA filing

On rera.karnataka.gov.in, open the project. Under section 4(2)(c) of the RERA Act, the promoter must file authenticated copies of the approvals and commencement certificate from the competent authority, and under 4(2)(d) the sanctioned plan. The authority named on those documents tells you a lot. If it's a city corporation or GBA document, that's one answer. If it's a panchayat or a regional planning authority, that's another.

2. Ask for the khata and latest property tax receipt

Ask the developer for the khata extract and the most recent property tax receipt for the project land. Note who issued them. For city properties, you can look up records on GBA's e-Aasthi portal. For panchayat properties, the record should be traceable on e-Swathu (here is how property tax is paid for a flat under a gram panchayat). Once you own the flat, see when property tax starts on a new flat in Bengaluru. For a panchayat record, see how to search Form 9 and Form 11B on e-Swathu.

3. Check the land record

On the Bhoomi land records portal, look at the RTC for the survey number. It shows the village and land details. On former farmland, check that the land conversion is in place. How to read the RTC, including kharab.

4. Look at the sanctioned plan letterhead

It sounds basic, but the sanction letter's issuing authority and reference number are a quick tell. Compare it with what the developer says verbally.

5. Ask the developer directly, in writing

"Is this project's land under a city corporation or a gram panchayat, and who sanctioned the building plan?" A straight question deserves a straight written answer.

A note on e-Aasthi 2.0

GBA launched e-Aasthi 2.0 in September 2026. According to GBA's announcement and press reports, it supports converting draft e-Khata to final e-Khata, issuing final e-Khata with mutation, Kaveri registration integration, and B-Khata to A-Khata conversion, with more modules being added in phases. If you're buying a resale or ready flat in a city area, it's worth checking the current e-Khata status there. For what the khata types mean, see A Khata, B Khata and e-Khata. After registration, khata transfer (mutation) updates that record into your name.

To look a flat up by its 10-digit Property ePID, see how to search a property on e-Aasthi.

What mistakes do buyers make?

  • Assuming a "Whitefield" or "Sarjapur Road" address means a city khata.
  • Relying on a developer's verbal answer without seeing the khata and tax receipt.
  • Not asking which authority sanctioned the plan.
  • Finding out about loan restrictions only when the bank's legal team reviews the file.
  • Assuming the position won't change. If you're told the area is "about to come under the city," treat that as unconfirmed until it's notified.

What should you check on any East fringe project?

  1. Open the project on Karnataka RERA and note who issued the approvals and plan sanction.
  2. Get the khata extract and latest property tax receipt from the developer.
  3. Look up the record on e-Aasthi (city) or e-Swathu (panchayat). For panchayat forms, see Form 11A vs Form 11B.
  4. Check the RTC on Bhoomi and the land conversion order if it was farmland.
  5. Ask your shortlisted lender whether they'll fund this project, before you pay a token. Add this to your pre-token document list.
  6. Have your lawyer confirm the position as part of the title review.

Frequently asked questions

Is a gram panchayat project in East Bangalore safe to buy?

It can be, if approvals, conversion and title are in order. The checks are different, and loan availability can vary, so do them before you commit.

Is BBMP still the right name to use?

Many people and older documents still say BBMP. The civic body is now organised under the Greater Bengaluru Authority with five city corporations, and its official site still sits at bbmp.gov.in.

Can I check this myself online?

Partly. The Karnataka RERA portal, e-Aasthi, e-Swathu and Bhoomi are all public. For certainty on a specific survey number, get your lawyer to confirm.

Does panchayat khata affect my home loan?

It can. Lenders have their own policies on khata type and approvals. Ask your lender early.

Will a panchayat area eventually come under the city?

Sometimes boundaries are expanded, but you shouldn't buy on that expectation. Treat it as unknown until it's formally notified.
